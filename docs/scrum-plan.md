# Orbit Plus — Scrum Development Plan

## Selected Development Model

Orbit Plus uses the Scrum framework because the system contains several interdependent role-based modules that can be planned, reviewed, and integrated as small usable increments.

Scrum allows the team to preserve the complete approved SRS in a prioritized Product Backlog while completing related requirements through short Sprints.

## Sprint Duration

Each Sprint lasts approximately one to two weeks.

## Scrum Roles

### Product Owner

The Product Owner is responsible for:

- Maintaining and prioritizing the Product Backlog.
- Clarifying requirement priorities.
- Confirming that completed work satisfies the intended business value.
- Accepting or returning work during Sprint Review.

### Scrum Master

The Scrum Master is responsible for:

- Facilitating Scrum events.
- Helping the team follow the agreed workflow.
- Identifying process obstacles.
- Supporting communication and coordination.
- Facilitating Sprint Retrospectives.

### Development Team

For this university project, the Development Team is responsible for:

- Requirement analysis.
- SRS documentation.
- Use-case and traceability preparation.
- Figma UI/UX design.
- Git documentation.
- Test-case preparation.
- Review and quality assurance.

## Scrum Events

### Sprint Planning

During Sprint Planning, the team:

- Defines the Sprint Goal.
- Selects prioritized Product Backlog items.
- Identifies dependencies.
- Assigns documentation and design tasks.
- Confirms acceptance criteria.

### Daily Scrum

The team uses short progress discussions to identify:

- Completed work.
- Current work.
- Problems or blockers.
- Work requiring review.

### Sprint Review

During the Sprint Review, completed documentation and Figma screens are evaluated against:

- Sprint Goal.
- User stories.
- Acceptance criteria.
- Functional requirements.
- Existing design-system rules.

Accepted feedback is recorded in the Product Backlog.

### Sprint Retrospective

The team reviews:

- What worked well.
- What caused confusion.
- Which tasks were delayed.
- How communication could improve.
- Which process changes should be used in the next Sprint.

## Definition of Done

A documentation or design item is considered complete when:

- It represents the approved SRS requirement.
- It does not introduce unsupported functionality.
- Its terminology is consistent.
- Its role permissions are correct.
- It is reviewed for obvious errors.
- Its requirement mapping is recorded.
- It is committed to the appropriate Git branch.
